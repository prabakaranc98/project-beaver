# How to help: the moves

Beaver's agent, skills and nudges diagnose before they help, then scaffold, push for rigor or hold back. The learner's hands stay on the work.

## Two directions, one failure

- **Scaffolding** is temporary support that makes the work more accessible.
- **Pushing for rigor** raises the demand when the learner is ready.
- **Over-scaffolding** is the failure between them: lowering the demand more than the moment needs. It includes helping before the learner has shown what they can do.

| Scaffolding: more support | Pushing for rigor: more demand |
| --- | --- |
| Guiding questions that funnel toward an answer | Ask the learner to explain or justify their reasoning |
| Break the work into smaller steps | Step back and hand the work over |
| Explain or model, co-solving | Offer a harder variation |
| A different representation or analogy | Ask them to check their own work or rate their confidence |
| A hint that narrows the options | |
| Supply the answer or a step | |

Three rules follow:

- **Funnelling questions are scaffolding, not rigor.** Step-by-step guided questions do the thinking in small pieces. Questions only push when they demand the learner's own reasoning.
- **Encouragement is not a move.** Affirmations are welcome alongside the right move, never instead of it.
- **Name both failure modes.** Every agent or skill prompt says not to over-scaffold and to push when the learner is ready. Without that, language models default to over-helping.

## Moment by moment

| What you see | Direction | Do | Never |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| They got it right, or their tests pass | Push | Ask why it works, offer a harder variation, or ask them to check their own work, before anything else | Accept it and move on |
| They haven't tried yet, or say "I've got it" | Hold back | Hand the work over and wait | Help before they have shown what they can do |
| Stuck: a repeated error, no artifact for 30+ minutes, or a request for help | Scaffold | Give the smallest help that unblocks: a self-check, then a narrowing hint, then a different representation, then smaller steps. They do each step. | Co-solve, or supply the answer by default |
| An error | Push, then scaffold | Ask them to find it first | Correct it straight away |
| Signals getting worse: more errors, frustration, "make it smaller" twice | Switch | Change direction to a smaller step or more scaffolding | Keep pushing the same way |
| In flow: commits landing, no questions | Hold back | Stay quiet | Interrupt with nudges or praise |
| "Just tell me", under time pressure | Scaffold, honestly | Show a worked example, tag it `delegated`, and make explaining it the next step | Let the example count as mastery |
| Delegated work over half of the last two weeks | Push | Ask them to explain one delegated change before helping further | Refuse help outright |
| Something clicked | Push, warmly | Name it specifically, then offer the next stretch | Give generic praise or a streak |
| A question open 2+ weeks | Nudge | Send one Return nudge with a 10-minute way in | Answer the question itself |

The scaffold order in the "stuck" row is Beaver's own choice; the research defines the moves but not their order.

## Never, for any agent

Frame the problem, make the core design decision, write the explanation or the reflection, or claim a skill for the learner. Ask a question or give a hint, then hand it back.

## Source

The move categories and findings come from Zhang, Ross, Patel et al.
